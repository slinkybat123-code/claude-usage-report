Privacy policy

What Limit stores, who can see it, and how to delete it.

Last updated 23 November 2026

The short version

  • Your figures are yours. Only you can see them, plus friends you both agreed to share with, and they see only totals and limits.
  • We never sell your data or share it with advertisers.
  • Ads appear only on public pages such as the guides. The pages you see after signing in have no ads and load no ad code.
  • You can delete your account and everything in it yourself, at any time.

What is stored

When you sign in with Google

Your name, email address and profile picture. Limit never sees your Google password.

From the app on your computer

The app reads the files Claude Code keeps on your computer and sends a summary to your account:

  • token counts, models and what they would cost at API prices, per day and per chat
  • for each chat: its title, the project folder it ran in (with your home folder replaced by ~), the git branch, and short excerpts (up to about 400 characters) of your first and latest message and of Claude's latest reply, so the dashboard can show what each chat was about
  • your plan-limit readings, if you added the reporter
  • similar totals from Codex CLI and Gemini CLI, if you use them
  • your computer's name and whether it runs Windows or macOS

It doesn't send your project files, whole chats or your Claude password.

Keys you add on All your AIs

If you add a key for OpenAI, OpenRouter or the Claude API, it's saved in your account so the page can fetch your usage. Only you can read it, plus your own linked computer for a Claude API key (that one is fetched by your computer). Google encrypts stored data, but the key itself is kept as you entered it so it can be used. You can remove a key at any time on the same page.

Friends

If you invite someone, their email address is stored with the invite. Once you both agree, each of you can see the other's card: name, picture, computer names, daily and monthly totals and plan limits. Never chat titles, excerpts or folders.

Where it's kept, and who can see it

Everything is stored in Google Firebase, which runs sign-in and the database for Limit. Access rules in the database decide who can read what: you, and the friends you accepted, as described above. The people who run Limit can technically reach the database to keep it working. They don't look at individual accounts unless you ask for help with yours.

Ads and cookies on the public pages

The home page, the guides and pages like this one may show ads from Google AdSense. Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on your earlier visits to this website or other websites. Google's use of advertising cookies lets it and its partners show you ads based on your visits to this and other sites.

  • You can turn off personalised ads in My Ad Center.
  • How Google uses information from sites that show its ads: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.
  • Visitors in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland are asked for consent before ads use cookies or personal data, and can change their choice at any time.

The signed-in pages and the download page load no ad code. Signing in keeps a sign-in token in your browser (that's how Firebase remembers you), and the site remembers a few settings, like the theme and which computer you picked. Limit uses no analytics or tracking of its own.

Deleting your data

  • Everything: sign in, open Computers and choose Delete my account. Your figures, keys, computers, friend card, shares and invites are removed, and so is your sign-in.
  • One computer: unlink it on the Computers page. Its figures are removed and it stops sending.
  • The app: uninstall it like any other app. Its small data folder (the link to your account) stays on your computer until you delete it.

Children

Limit isn't meant for children under 13, and they shouldn't use it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. Big changes will also be noted on the home page.
